{%- comment -%} Album: Michi's exhibit. Two shelves of Michi's album side by side: Upcoming, the trips to come, and Postmarked, the trips that are over, each stamp cancelled with its last stop's town and its last day. Five trips around the world, each printed in its own ink: Lisbon, Cape Town and Kyoto to come, Big Sur and Iceland's ring road over. The shelves share one row of six places, one more than there are stamps, each shelf as wide as its stamps: Upcoming from the left, Postmarked to the right, so the place between them is the one an empty shelf takes, a stamp's room to be dropped on. The stamps keep their size, and the album its height, whichever shelf they're on. Under the shelves, a line says what a stamp does when tapped or dragged. A press on a stamp postmarks it and files it on Postmarked, or takes its postmark off and files it back on Upcoming. A stamp dragged along its shelf is picked up and held over the page, the stamps it passes making way, then pressed into its new place, as in Michi; dragged onto the other shelf, it's postmarked as it's set down there, or has its postmark taken off. A drag sideways picks a stamp up, or one that rests on it a moment first; a drag up or down at once scrolls the page. From the keyboard, Enter or Space is a press, Alt with the left and right arrows moves a stamp along its shelf, and with up and down onto the other one. Each stamp leans a little, one way or the other by its place, as the album's do. Without JavaScript the stamps simply stand on their shelves. The drawings keep Michi's own proportions (StampView, StampArt and PostmarkView in the app): the perforation, frame and value tablet; the sea and big lakes hatched in horizontal lines inside a crisp coast; straight legs on a paper casing, a dot at each stop and a ring at the first; the postmark's rings, waves, lettering and worn ink. Coasts and lakes are Natural Earth's (public domain), Kyoto's rivers traced by hand, each framed as Michi frames a trip's stops. In dark mode a postmark runs off its stamp in the page's light ink, as Michi's do, and stays dark on the stamp. Titles are in the system serif, which is New York on Apple's devices, as in Michi. The defs the stamps share come first. One per page. Params class optional extra classes {%- endcomment -%}
